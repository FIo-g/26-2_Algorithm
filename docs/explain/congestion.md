The multipliers change edge travel times, and the synthetic traffic profiles are experimental assumptions, not measured traffic data.
For a 10-second edge, a multiplier of 2.0 gives 20 seconds, while 0.5 gives 5 seconds.
By default, random congestion selects about 30% of the edges and doubles their travel times.
Using the same edge ordering and random seed reproduces the same selection.
Each traffic scenario creates a new graph and leaves the original graph unchanged.
The speedup control halves travel times on all motorway and trunk edges, including their link edges, and on about 10% of the remaining edges.
If an edge becomes faster than its preprocessing value, the existing ALT heuristic may overestimate the remaining time and lose its optimality guarantee.
In the example path with edge times of 1, 1, and 2 seconds, the time-based midpoint is vertex 2, reached after 2 seconds.
